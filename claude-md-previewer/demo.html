Why the previewer reads well

      The Markdown viewer in the Claude desktop app is quiet by design. It sets one column of serif text at fifteen pixels, gives every line room to breathe, and keeps everything else (contents, notes, links) out of the way until you reach for it.1 This page rebuilds it with open fonts so the parts can be copied.

      Nothing here is clever on its own. What makes it work is that a dozen small decisions all point the same way: towards the text.

      Type

      The body face is a sturdy, large x-height serif with low contrast, the kind that holds up at small sizes on screen. Headings use the same family at a medium weight rather than bold, so a heading reads as a change of voice, not a shout. Literata is the closest open match; Source Serif 4 is a close second.

      Size and leading

      Body text is 15px on a 24px line (1.6). That is generous for a serif, and it is the single biggest reason long documents feel calm. The column stops at 36 times the body size, about 540px or seventy characters a line.

      Colour

      Text is a warm near-black on white, secondary text a warm grey. Links are a deep blue with an underline at 40% strength that firms up on hover. Every border and fill is the ink colour at 5, 10 or 20% opacity, which is why the page never looks busy.2

      Structure

      Space above a heading grows with its rank: three lines before a top heading, two and a half before a second-level one, two before a third. Space after a heading is only two thirds of a line, so each heading clearly belongs to the text below it.

      A blockquote keeps the body colour and size, marked only by a thin rule. It stays part of the argument instead of fading into grey.

      • Lists indent by two ems, with grey markers.
      • Items are a third of a line apart, so a list reads as one block.
      • Nested lists change marker: disc, circle, square.

      Code

      Inline code such as config.json sits in a faint tinted box two pixels smaller than the text, in the body colour rather than red. Blocks get a soft border and a 9px radius.

      .prose {
  font-size: 15px;
  line-height: 1.6;
  max-width: calc(15px * 36);
}

      Navigation

      On the left, in the gutter, is a rail of short ticks, one per heading. Ticks are longer for higher-level headings, darker once you have read past them, and blue for where you are. Move the pointer onto the rail and the full outline opens; click a heading to go there.

      Footnotes

      Click a footnote number and the note opens in a small card under it, so you can read it without losing your place.3 The card has one button, "Jump to note", which scrolls down to the notes and briefly highlights the one you came for.

      What to borrow

      The measure, the leading, the heading scale and the footnote card are the parts worth copying first. The tick rail is the most distinctive, and the most work.
